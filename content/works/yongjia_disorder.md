The original is left in Classical Chinese; the English version below is a reading aid and set of notes, not a replacement for the poem.

## Original

> 衣冠挈族过江渚，中原膏血饲群胡。
>
> 河洛烽烟燔帝邑，白骨横斜叠巷隅。

## English Rendering

> The cap-and-gown families led their clans across the river isles;
>
> the Central Plain's wealth and blood fed the hordes of Hu.
>
> Beacon fires along the He and Luo burned the imperial city;
>
> white bones lay aslant, heaped in the alley corners.

## Notes

- **七古（qigu）** is a seven-character poem in the older, less tightly regulated ancient style. The English title renders it as “old-style verse” because it is not a strict regulated quatrain or regulated octave.
- **永嘉丧乱** refers to the catastrophe of the Yongjia era (307–313) of the Western Jin dynasty. Its central event was the fall of Luoyang in 311 to the Han-Zhao forces; Chang'an fell in 316, ending effective Western Jin rule in the north.
- **衣冠** literally means “caps and gowns,” the dress of officials and scholars. Here it stands for the elite and established families.
- **过江渚** evokes crossing southward across the Yangzi region. This was the beginning of the phrase **衣冠南渡**—“the southward crossing of the cap-and-gown families”—which later described the migration of northern elites and institutions to the south.
- **膏血** is literally “fat and blood”: the wealth, sustenance, and lifeblood extracted from a population. The line compresses both plunder and slaughter into one image.
- **群胡** is a Jin-period Han Chinese term for the northern peoples and armed groups, especially in the context of the upheavals associated with the “Five Hu.” Its force here is polemical and historical rather than a neutral modern ethnonym.
- **河洛** pairs the Yellow River (河) and the Luo River (洛), a conventional way to name the Central Plains and the old imperial heartland.
- **帝邑** means the imperial capital, referring above all to Luoyang, then the Western Jin seat of government.
- The final couplet moves from strategic collapse to human remains: **白骨横斜叠巷隅** turns political catastrophe into a street-level image of bones heaped at the corners of lanes.
